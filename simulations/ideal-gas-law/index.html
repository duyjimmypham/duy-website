Ideal Gas Laws

This simulation illustrates the relationships among pressure, volume, temperature, and amount of gas in an ideal-gas model.

Kinetic Molecular Theory Kinetic molecular theory relates microscopic particle motion to the macroscopic variables P, V, T, and n.
Animated gas chamber representing the current particle distribution, piston position, and pressure.
Graph of the selected gas-law relationship. Current values are reported in the labeled controls.

Gas variables

P
Pressure (atm): the force per unit area exerted on the container walls as a result of particle collisions.
V
Volume (L): the space occupied by the gas, represented by the region beneath the piston.
T
Temperature (K): a measure of the average translational kinetic energy of the gas particles.
n
Amount of gas (mol): the quantity of gas expressed in moles.

Ideal-gas model

  • Gas particles move continuously in random directions.
  • Collisions of the particles with the container walls and to each other are elastic.
  • The total particle volume is negligible compared with the volume of the container.
  • Intermolecular attractions and repulsions are negligible.
V is recalculated when P changes.
