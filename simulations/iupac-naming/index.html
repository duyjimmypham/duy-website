      About these names

      Names follow the IUPAC 2013 Recommendations (the Blue Book) and give the preferred IUPAC name. Two of its rules differ from many older textbooks: the longest chain is chosen before the chain with the most double or triple bonds, and a ring outranks a chain. The questions only use structures where the old and new rules pick the same parent, so either textbook gives the same answer.

      • Locants sit next to the part they number: but-2-ene, propan-2-ol. The older forms 2-butene and 2-propanol are accepted when you type.
      • IUPAC keeps formic acid, acetic acid, formaldehyde, acetaldehyde and acetamide as preferred names; methanoic acid, ethanoic acid, methanal, ethanal and ethanamide are accepted too.
      • Branched substituents use the preferred prefixes propan-2-yl, butan-2-yl, 2-methylpropyl and tert-butyl; isopropyl, sec-butyl and isobutyl are accepted.
      • Skeletal drawings show no wedges, so ring cis/trans and R/S are not part of these names. In the E/Z tier the drawing fixes each double bond’s geometry.
